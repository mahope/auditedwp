# Post #2 — Graveyard gaps are the WORST opportunities

## Dead products with live demand look like the perfect opening. I tested five. Here's the autopsy of the autopsies.

The most seductive opportunity signal in indie SaaS is the shutdown: a real product, a real
user base, proven willingness to pay — and nobody serving them anymore. "Find the graveyard,
build the replacement." It was my method #14. It failed in all five tests, faster than any
other approach.

### The five graves

- **GummySearch** (Reddit research tool, closed Nov 2025 after Reddit's API licensing
  change). 135,000+ users orphaned. Within months: at least eight replacements — RedNudge
  ($7–19/mo), Shadow Inbox ($49–249/mo), Insightios, Syften and more — all carrying the
  same platform risk that killed the original.
- **Shopify Stocky** (native PO app, closed Aug 2026). Seven replacement apps live or in
  beta six weeks after the announcement — several disclosed by hobby builders *inside the
  announcement thread itself.*
- **InkFrog** (eBay listings, closed Jun 2026). The migration market was fully occupied
  before the shutdown date: Snap2List, 3Dsellers, Salestio, DashVue — with SEO content
  ranking on "inkfrog alternative" before the funeral.
- **Chartable** (podcast attribution, closed Dec 2024). One year later, industry panels
  conclude "the measurement gap has largely closed": Podkite, Podstatus, Podgagement,
  CoHost filled it.
- **Coverfly / Tracking Board / Done Deal Pro** (screenwriting ecosystem, 2025). Even the
  one feature migration guides called "the genuine gap nobody is talking about" — buyer
  intelligence — had a dedicated live product (ScriptMatch) when I checked.

### Why graveyards are the worst, not best

A shutdown obituary is the loudest possible opportunity broadcast. Every builder reads the
same migrations guides and Hacker News threads. Swarm latency: **6 weeks to saturation**
(Stocky), and even SEO around a not-yet-dead product is pre-owned. You're not finding a gap;
you're arriving late to a riot.

Worse: the original often died for structural reasons you inherit. GummySearch died on
platform risk — so does every clone. If the ground killed the last tenant, check the ground.

### What graveyards ARE good for

Two things. First, they prove demand exists in a category — useful context, useless as an
opening. Second, they define what winning actually requires: being first to the obituary,
which requires a standing listening post and distribution already in place. Discovery speed
is an infrastructure problem, not a research problem.

That's why my own operation now runs a permanent watch on shutdowns and deprecations — and
why nothing gets built from a signal until it passes four criteria within days, not months:
proven payment, fewer than two competitors on deep dig, a moat reachable from day one,
and an idea too complex to fit in one blog post.
