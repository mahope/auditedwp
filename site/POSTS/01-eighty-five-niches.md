# Post #1 — Hovedautopsien

## 85 niches. 14 methods. Zero openings. Here's the pattern nobody publishes.

Over the past weeks I ran a one-person, zero-budget research operation looking for ONE
business idea worth building. Not brainstorming — actual market forensics: competitor digs,
pricing structures, distribution-cost estimates, buyer analysis. Every candidate had to pass
one bar before I'd write a line of code: *would I put my own money in it?*

**85+ candidates across 14 different research methods. None survived.**

That's not the interesting part. The interesting part is WHY they died — because the reason
was the same every single time, and nobody building in public seems to know it.

### What I tested

- Compliance waves: EAA, CSRD/VSME, CBAM, EUDR, DORA, California ADMT, IRS FIRE→IRIS
- Vertical ops pain: dental insurance verification, HOA minutes, construction pay apps,
  COI tracking, lien waivers, permit expediting, Amazon seller ops, Stripe reconciliation
- "Sell shovels" plays, agent-economy infrastructure (MCP/x402), graveyard gaps
  (GummySearch, Shopify Stocky, InkFrog, Chartable), language arbitrage (JP/DE),
  litigation-data mining (Schedule A mass suits)

### The pattern

Every niche with documented, visible demand was already occupied by **5–12 competitors
within 6–12 months of the signal appearing** — often faster:

- Regulation announced → software swoop lands before the deadline
- Product shuts down → replacement apps live within **6 weeks** of the announcement
- Pain visible on Reddit → someone in the thread is already marketing their own prototype

The correlation was perfect: **the stronger the demand signal, the denser the competition.**
Signal strength doesn't find gaps for you. It tells you where everyone else is already digging.

### Where the money actually sits

In every dead candidate, real money existed — but in the *service layer*, not the SaaS layer:
manual audits at $1,250–2,750 per asset while scanners sell at $0.10/URL; pay-app prep at
$300/document while software costs $49/mo; human verification at $11/unit while APIs undercut
everyone. The margin lives in responsibility, relationships and domain authority — things you
can't buy with build hours.

### The uncomfortable conclusion

Web research can prove a market exists. It cannot find unoccupied markets — because anything
findable by search is findable by the hundreds of other builders reading the same guides,
subreddits and obituaries. If your idea can be summarized in one blog post, assume someone
already shipped it.

I'll be publishing the autopsies — each failed niche, the data, why it died. And I keep a
live watch on fresh shutdowns and deprecations, evaluated against four criteria *before* the
swarm arrives. Follow along if you're about to build idea #86.
