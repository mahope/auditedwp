# Post #3 — The 6-week swarm

## From announcement to saturation: dissecting the fastest market fill I've ever measured

On June 15, 2026, Shopify announced it was shutting down Stocky, its native purchase-order
and reorder app for POS merchants. Native integration, free, used by thousands of small
retailers. Textbook "orphaned demand."

What happened next is the single best-documented case of what I now call the swarm: the
speed at which a visible gap fills once announced.

### The timeline

- **Weeks 1–2:** Established inventory platforms (Prediko, Sumtracker, Fabrikatör — all
  existing products) pivot their positioning toward Stocky migrants. Migration guides appear.
- **Weeks 3–4:** Purpose-built replacements ship: Stockful at $19.99/mo, Sensible Forecasting
  at $29, SKUSavvy with a dedicated importer. A free-tier entrant (Replenora) appears.
- **Weeks 5–6:** Hobby builders self-disclose their prototypes *inside the official
  announcement thread* — competing with each other in the comments of the funeral.

Six weeks. From "nobody serves this" to a price ladder from $0 to $49+/mo with at least
seven options. The bottom of the market was saturated before the old product actually died.

### The thread participant who said it out loud

One builder in the thread wrote the most honest sentence I found in my entire research:

> "It is the loudest gap, so it is the one everybody is aiming at. The quieter gaps have
> less competition."

He was right about the mechanism. He was also, himself, already selling a product into one
of those "quieter gaps." That's the recursion that makes this game brutal: everyone hunting
quiet gaps is noise for the next hunter. Quietness decays fast.

### What this means for your launch

1. Any gap visible in an announcement thread, changelog, Reddit post or buyer's guide has
   a swarm latency measured in weeks, not months.
2. Your only durable advantages at gap-discovery are speed (a real-time listening post)
   and distribution you already own. Discovery alone is worth nothing in 2026.
3. If your go-to-market plan starts with "build it and then figure out distribution,"
   you are planning to arrive last to a six-week race.
