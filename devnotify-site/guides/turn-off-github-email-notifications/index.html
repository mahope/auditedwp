How to turn off GitHub email notifications

Updated August 2026 · 4 min read

If your inbox fills up with "Re: [org/repo] PR #1234" every morning, you have three realistic options: turn everything off, turn most of it off, or replace the channel entirely. All three are covered below.

Option 1: Turn off all notification emails (nuclear)

  1. Go to GitHub → Settings → Notifications (github.com/settings/notifications).
  2. Under Email notification preferences, uncheck "Notifications worth reading" / all email delivery options.
  3. Scroll down and under Subscriptions, consider also hitting "Unsubscribe from all current subscriptions" — this clears the watching list that generates most of the volume.

This stops the flood immediately. It also stops you hearing about anything: review requests, direct @mentions, security alerts. If your team relies on email for those, expect a "did you see my request?" message within days.

Option 2: Keep only what's about you (recommended)

The same settings page lets you be surgical instead:

  • In "Participating" vs "Watching": set your default email routing so you're only emailed for threads you participate in — not everything in repos you watch.
  • Under Customize by activity, disable email for issues, PRs, releases, discussions and dependabot alerts you don't care about.
  • Go through Watching repos list (github.com/watching) and hit Unwatch on repos you watch out of habit. This alone removes most of the noise for many people.
  • Per-repo override: on any repo, click Watch → Participating and @mentions to keep an important repo fully visible while silencing the rest.

Option 3: Replace the channel — see notifications natively instead

Email was never a good inbox for code events: it's sorted by time, not priority, and one urgent review request looks identical to forty CI emails. The alternative is to let GitHub's own categorization do the filtering and surface the result somewhere you actually look.

On macOS, that means the menu bar: an app showing your unread notification count next to the clock, where clicking any entry opens the exact issue or pull request. Because it reads your notifications feed with a personal access token, it shows precisely the things you'd have wanted emailed — mentions, review requests, threads you participate in — and nothing else. Everything stays local on your machine; no cloud relay.

You can then turn email off completely (Option 1) and lose nothing that matters.

Quick reference

  • All emails gone, accept the risk: Option 1 — two minutes.
  • Keep participating-only emails: Option 2 — ten minutes of settings + repo unwatching.
  • Better channel entirely: Option 3 — menu bar app, then kill email.
