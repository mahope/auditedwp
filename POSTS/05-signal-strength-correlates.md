# Post #5 — Stronger signals, more competitors

## The inverse rule of opportunity hunting: signal strength predicts competition density. Perfectly.

After 14 different research methods and ~85 niches, one relationship held without a single
exception. Rank any demand signal by strength, and competition density rises in lockstep:

**Level 1 — Complaints (weakest signal → thinnest competition... supposedly)**
Reddit threads about HOA boards tracking violations in Gmail labels. But even complaint-
level visibility attracted PayHOA, ManageCasa, CINC and a hobbyist marketing his own
Lovable-built prototype in the comment replies.

**Level 2 — Buyer's guides exist**
Dental insurance verification: six-plus dedicated platforms ($200–500/mo) plus outsourced
services at $3–12.50 per check. COI tracking: twelve platforms reviewed and compared.
Construction lien waivers: top-13 listicles. If a guide exists, the swoop finished.

**Level 3 — People are paid per unit**
The strongest signal I learned to mine: job listings where companies hire humans for
repetitive digital work. Amazon catalog fixes: freelancers at $20–108/ASIN, productized
$500–2,500/mo retainers, software at $19/mo — every micro-segment occupied. Permit
expediting: $400–15,000 per permit, plus a $91M-funded startup (PermitFlow). Restaurant
invoice entry: already cheaper done by AI than offshore labor.

**Level 4 — Dead products with live users (loudest → densest, always)**
Five graveyards tested; five saturated markets found; fastest fill: six weeks.

### The mechanism

All builders use overlapping discovery channels: Reddit, buyer's guides, acquisition
listings, shutdown announcements. So the population of hunters scales with the visibility
of the signal. You are never mining alone — every strong signal is a crowded dig site.

### Two practical consequences

1. **Weak signals aren't safe either.** They're weak precisely because there's no money
   there yet — or no way to reach the buyer. My four dead finalists all sold to people
   with low payment capacity (short-term-rental hosts, freelancers, micro-shops): strong
   complaints, empty wallets.

2. **The only asymmetries left are non-searchable:** proprietary data accumulated over
   years, domain authority, licenses, physical presence, relationships, or an audience you
   built before you needed it. Notice that none of these can be acquired by research hours.
   They must be built first — which is exactly why I'm building a distribution asset now,
   in public, before choosing what to sell through it.
